MEASUREMENT, WITH CONTEXT

Know what the numbers mean.

The assumptions behind every POLAR result.

01 / REFLECTIONS

Known-volume contact angle

POLAR detects a complete ring of 24 LED reflections, pairs opposite centroids and solves your telecentric reflection and spherical-cap volume equations. Distances use the original image pixels.

Focus selection compares the spread of light around the LEDs. Incomplete rings are rejected. A scan-boundary selection is marked for review.

02 / GEOMETRY

Outline + LEDs

A circular outline alone cannot determine an unknown angle and volume. Combining it with the LED geometry provides an experimental spherical-cap estimate.

Below 90°, the visible edge can be the contact line. Above 90°, the silhouette is the sphere's widest section. POLAR handles these separately and rejects ambiguous or impossible solutions. You must confirm the physical boundary.

03 / ERROR

Two contributions, clearly defined

The reported error combines directional sample SD and the geometric effect of a uniformly distributed focus-height offset of ± half a height step. A 0.2 mm step gives u(Z) = 0.057735 mm.

These contributions are assumed uncorrelated. The result excludes volume, calibration, boundary fitting and model errors. It is not a 95% interval or the standard error of the mean.

Uncertainty propagation ↗
04 / SURFACE ENERGY

OWRK, with identifiable inputs

POLAR requires three distinct liquids with sufficient polar/dispersive contrast. It fits y = b + mx, then γˢᵈ = b² and γˢᵖ = m², using nonnegative coefficients.

Use one substrate preparation and angle convention. Temperature is recorded only; the calculation uses the liquid properties entered in the table without automatic temperature correction. Negative unconstrained coefficients are flagged. Entered angular spreads propagate through 20,000 repeated fits. Reported errors are one SD, conditional on the inputs and model, not total measurement uncertainty. Liquid-property uncertainties and correlations between liquids are not included.
